# Parla public launch plan

Research date: September 21, 2026. This is a research and execution plan, not a record of completed promotion. No social posts, outreach, purchases, or advertisements were made for this document. Proposed targets are planning thresholds, not forecasts or industry benchmarks.

The recommendation is to launch Parla around **local dictation that respects your wording**, demonstrate the improvement with real recordings, and earn repeat use before increasing reach. Optional stutter cleanup, faster completion, and additional operating systems can strengthen that promise once each has release evidence. GitHub stars are a secondary measure of developer interest.

## What the deeper OpenWhispr research changes

OpenWhispr did deliberately request stars, but its public record does not establish purchased or automated stars. It accumulated an audience over roughly 15 months. Search pages, repeated founder demonstrations, developer communities, and later Trending exposure are documented. The proportion of stars attributable to each remains unknown.

Three findings are particularly useful for Parla:

1. **The search operation is larger than a few blog posts.** A direct count of its public sitemap on September 21 found 98 canonical URLs: 43 use-case detail pages, 17 comparison detail pages, 15 blog articles, and 23 other pages including their indexes. The homepage entry advertises ten language variants, including English; this does not prove every translation is complete or indexed. This is an acquisition surface across problems, competitors, platforms, and languages. Page counts do not establish traffic or conversion. [Sitemap](https://openwhispr.com/sitemap.xml)
2. **Its present growth stack extends beyond organic founder posting.** The September 11 privacy policy lists Google Analytics/Ads measurement, RB2B business visitor identification, mobile AppsFlyer attribution, and Superwall paywall experiments. That documents disclosed capabilities, not spend, campaign performance, or a cause of past stars. The founder's February organic-search report should not be generalized to all subsequent acquisition. [Privacy policy](https://openwhispr.com/privacy)
3. **Distribution did not remove conversion problems.** In February, the founder reported roughly 1,300–1,400 stars and 1,000 account signups in 11 days, but only about 30 paying customers. He explicitly distinguished nearly 50,000 cumulative GitHub downloads from an estimated 15,000–20,000 unique downloads because updates inflated the former. His stated product response was easier setup and additional paid value. These are self-reported, contemporaneous numbers, not independently audited cohorts. [Founder update](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gabrielste1n_openwhispr-just-crossed-1000-sign-ups-in-activity-7429997863952097281-SqR5)

Parla should borrow the clarity, demonstrations, search intent, and repeated useful releases. It should not assume that copying a broad feature list or asking for stars produces the same outcome.

## The acquisition mechanism to test

The observed material supports this hypothesis:

```text
Recognizable problem or competitor search
    -> use-case/comparison page or founder demonstration
    -> clear download and GitHub links
    -> first useful dictation
    -> continued use, feedback and voluntary recommendation
    -> fresh release material and occasional curator/Trending exposure
```

The first four steps are within Parla's control. Earned coverage is an outcome to support, not a promised result. Keep download and source links distinct: a user who wants to dictate should not have to understand a repository first.

OpenWhispr's developer page does something more specific than saying “AI productivity”: it describes prompts, reviews, commit messages, and terminal workflows. Its FAQ also distinguishes dictating prose around code from full voice programming. Parla can use the same level of specificity with its own measured behavior. [Developer use case](https://openwhispr.com/use-cases/developers)

## Positioning and claims for Parla

The initial audience should be people who dictate long prompts or everyday writing and want control over how much cleanup occurs. A second, separately evaluated audience is people who want optional removal of unwanted repetitions. Ask those users to shape the behavior; do not assume every repeated word is an error or every speaker wants it removed.

| Message | Why it could matter | Evidence required before publication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Dictate in your own words.” | A clear alternative to aggressive rewriting. | Demonstrations and tests of Faithful behavior; explain that recognition itself can make errors. |
| “Optional cleanup for repeated speech.” | Makes the new correction feature discoverable without portraying a speech difference as something to cure. | Opt-in control, before/after examples, protected names/numbers/negations, preservation of intentional repetition, and a recovery path. |
| “Local dictation, with your models included.” | Makes setup concrete. | Exact release manifest; distinguish a bundled installer from packages that download models separately. |
| “Text appears in [measured interval] after you stop.” | Addresses the actual waiting period. | End-to-end benchmark including finalization and insertion, disclosed hardware/model/clip duration and cold versus warm runs. |
| “Windows, macOS and Linux.” | Makes the product usable across a person's machines. | Published, installed and tested artifacts for the specific architectures and desktop environments listed. Compiling a core library is insufficient. |
| “Public source on GitHub.” | Lets people inspect implementation and contribute feedback. | Working source/build links. Use “open source” only after the maintainer establishes an appropriate app license and preserves dependency notices. |

At this research checkpoint, the last documented published baseline was Windows `0.2.0-bundled-20260919`, with Parakeet and Whisper included. The Windows handoff reported an unsigned installer, checks on the development machine, and no clean Windows VM validation. The older synced README was stale. Work in this release session may supersede those facts; use [current state](current-state.md), [README](../README.md), release checksums, and actual platform test records when approving copy.

Do not call Parla “the fastest,” “perfectly accurate,” “a treatment for stuttering,” “compatible with every app,” or “fully private” without defining the relevant behavior. For local operation, explain model installation, optional services, local history, retry audio, and any diagnostics. Do not borrow OpenWhispr's medical/compliance claims or promise a future price model.

A comparison page should compare versioned facts, acknowledge where OpenWhispr is more capable, and link to its official documentation. The useful differentiation is controlled wording, setup, and measured completion speed in selected workflows. Meeting bots, mobile apps, agents, or enterprise features are not required to make that case.

## Release gates before a broad launch

These are marketing release gates; they do not replace engineering checks or prevent publishing clearly labeled preview builds.

| Gate | Evidence to attach | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Install and update | Fresh-machine install, first dictation, restart, update, uninstall and data-preservation checks for each promoted OS/architecture. State Windows signing and macOS signing/notarization status accurately. | Engineering |
| Linux scope | Name tested distributions, audio stack, X11/Wayland desktop and insertion permissions; distinguish automatic insertion from clipboard fallback. | Engineering |
| Wording and stutter cleanup | Versioned corpus including names, identifiers, dates, negations, natural repeated emphasis, false starts, and voluntarily supplied disfluent speech. Record intended text and false deletions; any change of protected meaning blocks that example/claim. | Engineering + beta participants |
| Speed | At least 30 repeated trials for each published hardware/model condition; report p50/p95 completion delay, recognition error/edits, cold start, and memory. Compare identical input with identical cleanup settings where possible. | Engineering |
| Recovery and support | A failed dictation has an understandable recovery route; no accidental form submission; known limitations and issue template are accessible. | Engineering |
| Distribution | Public downloads resolve anonymously, hashes match, version labels agree, size is stated, and support links work. | Release owner |
| Licensing and attribution | App license decision is explicit; any reused OpenWhispr code retains required notices; included model/runtime redistribution terms are documented. | Maintainer |
| Messaging | Every headline is tied to evidence from the actual release, with unsupported platforms/features labeled preview or omitted. | Founder |

For the beta, target ten observed installation sessions per promoted platform and at least five consenting participants who want repetition cleanup. These are useful discovery samples, not proof of population-wide reliability. Invite feedback on preferred wording and allow participants to keep recordings private. No public testimonial or recording without permission.

## First 30 days: prove the promise and launch a useful beta

Day 1 begins when the release owner has an identifiable candidate build. If a gate fails, continue research, documentation and repair; move the public launch date rather than implying an unfinished feature is ready.

| Window | Concrete work | Deliverable / decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–3 | Confirm release matrix, claims, app license and download path. Measure installer friction and cold/warm dictation. Define the two cleanup modes in user language. | One-page evidence sheet and honest download page. |
| Days 4–7 | Observe the first ten consenting beta users completing their own task. Recruit through existing relationships or explicitly permitted channels. Record install failures and unwanted edits without collecting transcript content by default. | Ranked list of the five biggest obstacles; repair the top two. |
| Days 8–10 | Record three continuous demonstrations: a coding prompt, everyday writing with deliberate repetition, and offline operation. Build a 30-second cut and a full annotated version of each. | Six assets, each labeled with build, model and hardware. |
| Days 11–14 | Prepare README, screenshots, release notes, download guide, privacy explanation, comparison methodology, and a short FAQ from beta questions. Publish two useful search pages when ready. | Download-to-first-dictation route reviewed by an unfamiliar tester. |
| Days 15–21 | Soft launch on the founder's existing social accounts and one relevant community where sharing is permitted. Answer questions, fix failures, and capture source attribution. | 25 external activated testers as a planning target; two user-validated workflow examples. |
| Days 22–30 | Make one broader launch after first-week beta retention is measurable. Use a human-written Show HN submission if eligible, or a maker launch appropriate to the audience. Avoid stacking every channel on the same day. | Channel-level visit/download/activation report and week-one retention, with denominators and unknowns. |

Allocate approximately eight founder hours per week: three for users/support, two for demos, two for useful pages and distribution, one for measurement. Engineering repair time is separate. Do not sacrifice support to meet a content quota.

## Days 31–60: find a repeatable channel

- Ship one meaningful improvement each week or two, and show the exact user problem it resolves. A release is a reason to communicate when the behavior changes, not just when a version increments.
- Maintain two distinct acquisition experiments at a time: developer workflow demonstrations and high-intent search pages. Limit cross-platform expansion in copy to the verified release matrix.
- Publish the first reproducible completion-time benchmark. Include disappointing cases and the measurement script or methodology; compare edit burden alongside speed.
- Add four substantive pages selected from the search backlog below. Update existing pages before creating overlapping variants.
- Ask retained users for an optional workflow story or recommendation. Offer a shareable download link; do not make benefits depend on stars or praise.
- After actual use supports a story, propose one relevant meetup demonstration and a handful of individualized curator submissions. This is future human outreach, not something executed by this plan.
- Planning target: 75 activated users in total and at least 20 observable week-one returns. If activation or return is weak, use this period to fix the product and narrow the audience rather than buying reach.

## Days 61–90: expand what has earned repeat use

- Compare channels by retained users per founder hour, with acquisition cost only where money was spent. Choose one primary channel and one supporting channel.
- Prepare Product Hunt only if the installation experience, screenshots, demo and support capacity are ready. It is a discovery event, not a substitute for a durable acquisition channel.
- Translate the two strongest pages into one language only after user demand, actual speech support, and a competent reviewer justify it. Ten locale routes are not ten proven markets.
- Make the strongest verified platform release a fresh launch event; publish clear compatibility instructions and a real demonstration from that OS.
- If retention is sufficient and an owner approves spending, run one capped paid experiment. Do not hire a growth creator or scale paid campaigns on GitHub-star growth alone.
- Planning target: 150 activated users, an observable group of 40 returning users, and one channel that delivers useful users repeatedly. Rebase those goals against the first month's evidence; they are not predictions.

## Launch sequence and assets

1. **Before announcing:** download every public installer as an anonymous visitor, verify hashes and the source tag, test the landing page on a second machine, and put known issues beside the download.
2. **Soft launch:** share the strongest real demonstration with the founder's existing audience. Link to a download page with a separate source link. Be available for installation questions.
3. **First 48 hours:** keep a public known-issues note current. Resolve launch blockers and tell affected users exactly which build fixes them.
4. **Community launch:** share a relevant technical or workflow account where the community permits it. Explain the maker relationship and existing limitations. Recheck current community rules at posting time.
5. **One week later:** publish what users taught us, a fixed problem, and a new demonstration. Show factual progress, including failure cases; do not invent traction.
6. **Later broader launch:** use Product Hunt or a platform-specific release when the experience merits another introduction. A single launch day should not carry the whole plan.

For Hacker News, the founder must write the submission and replies personally. Current rules prohibit generated or AI-edited text and requests for votes, comments or submissions. Use this document only as a factual checklist: original motivation, implementation choices, what can be tried, measured results, known limitations, and an honest question that arises naturally from the work. A waitlist alone is not a Show HN experience. [HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html), [Show HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)

For Product Hunt, prepare a personal maker account, two or more clear screenshots, an accurate pricing/status label, and a non-private YouTube demonstration if using video. The founder can launch directly; purchasing a hunter is unnecessary. Share the launch naturally without incentivized or coordinated votes. [Posting guide](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/479557-how-to-post-a-product), [sharing rules](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/2690626-how-do-i-share-my-post)

For Reddit and other communities, read the specific community's current rules. A general self-promotion allowance is not permission everywhere. Do not post identical promotion across many communities, scrape members, or send unsolicited mass messages. For speech-related communities, begin with listening and an opt-in research invitation only where moderators permit it. ## Draft copy and demonstration briefs

These are draft assets for the website and channels that permit assisted drafting. Verify every claim against the released build. Bracketed details must be filled with evidence before use. They are not ready-to-post Hacker News text.

**Website hero**

> Dictate in your own words.
>
> Parla turns speech into text on your computer. Choose how much cleanup you want, then review the words before you send them.
>
> Download for [verified OS] · See a real demonstration · View the source

**Optional feature announcement, only after validation**

> We added optional cleanup for repeated speech in Parla. This recording shows what I said, what the recognizer heard, and exactly what cleanup changed. You can turn it off. We are testing cases where repetition is intentional, because removing the wrong word can change the meaning. [Build and demo]

**Developer demonstration caption**

> Here is a complete [duration]-second prompt dictated into [tested application] with Parla [version]. This runs with [model] on [hardware]. The recording includes the wait after I stop speaking. Setup, limitations and download: [link].

**Voluntary GitHub CTA, used sparingly**

> If Parla is useful to you, a GitHub star helps others discover the project. Bug reports and honest feedback help us improve it.

Place that CTA in the README, release notes, or a useful founder update. Do not interrupt dictation with repeated prompts, gate downloads behind a star, offer rewards for stars, or coordinate voting. GitHub stars are bookmarks and expressions of interest, not endorsements of measured quality. On HN and Product Hunt, follow their own participation rules instead of transplanting this CTA into vote solicitation.

| Demo | Continuous recording should show | What to measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long coding prompt | Hotkey, speech, completion, insertion into a named tested app, and any necessary manual edit. Include a realistic identifier. | Time from speech end to usable text; edits required. |
| Repetition cleanup | Consenting speaker or clearly labeled synthetic test; raw recognition beside cleaned output; intentional repeated emphasis as a control. | False deletions, retained meaning, and user preference. Never fabricate a testimonial. |
| Offline operation | Models installed, network disconnected, complete dictation; disclose any setup/download dependency. | Successful completion and cold/warm difference. |
| Platform release | Installer to first dictation on the actual OS; show permission prompts and fallback behavior. | Setup time and failed steps, not just the successful final screen. |

## Measurement without collecting people's dictation

Use a simple funnel: **qualified page visit -> download click -> completed installation -> first successful dictation -> return in the following week -> voluntary recommendation**. A release download is not necessarily an installation; an update is not a new user; a star is not a retained user.

| Metric | Definition | Collection and caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified visit | Human visit to a use-case, comparison or download page from a recorded source. | Aggregate website measurement; identify bots and direct/unknown traffic separately. |
| Download intent | Click on the correct platform's artifact link. | Website event; GitHub asset counts are a separate aggregate and include updates/retries. |
| Activation | New consenting participant completes three successful dictations within seven days of first use. | Opt-in content-free event or explicit beta check-in. Keep denominator as observed new participants, not all downloads. |
| Week-one return | Activated participant dictates on at least two distinct days during days 8–14 after first use. | Report only eligible cohorts and disclose opt-in selection bias. |
| Completion time | Speech end to final usable text at destination. | Local performance counters, aggregated only with consent; label fallback-to-clipboard separately. |
| Cleanup quality | Unwanted changes and manual edits in the consented evaluation sample. | Local review or voluntarily submitted cases. Never upload transcripts automatically for marketing. |
| Recommendation | Voluntary share/referral and, separately, net GitHub star change. | Do not try to identify anonymous users by matching GitHub accounts. |
| Channel efficiency | Returning observed users divided by founder hours; money per activated/returning user when relevant. | Small samples are directional. List incomplete attribution rather than filling gaps. |

UTM convention for the future landing page:

```text
utm_source=linkedin|youtube|reddit|producthunt|newsletter_name|meetup_name
utm_medium=organic_social|video|community|referral|event|paid
utm_campaign=parla_public_beta_YYYYMM
utm_content=developer_demo_v1|cleanup_demo_v1|offline_demo_v1
```

Use lowercase values and a campaign sheet with publication date, URL, version, audience, owner, visits, download clicks, observed activations, eligible returns, hours and spend. Store campaign labels without names or spoken content. Passing UTMs to a GitHub release does not create reliable installer-level attribution. If website-to-app attribution is unavailable, use an optional “Where did you hear about Parla?” answer and report it separately.

Start with consented beta check-ins and aggregate website data; add product analytics only with a clear user choice and documented data flow. Do not adopt visitor identity enrichment merely because a competitor has it. A privacy-oriented product benefits from measuring the minimum necessary.

Review every Friday: compare cohorts, read failures, select one bottleneck, and choose next week's experiment. Suggested investigation thresholds are less than 60% activation among at least 20 observed new participants, or less than 30% week-one return among at least 20 eligible activated participants. These are internal decision rules, not market benchmarks. Low sample sizes call for interviews, not confident percentage claims.

## Budget-light experiment queue

| Experiment | Effort / cash cap | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developer prompt demo versus everyday-writing demo | Two matched founder posts and four hours; no media spend. | Compare qualified clicks and observed activations after seven days; repeat the stronger use case before declaring a winner. |
| Unedited timing demo versus polished explainer | Four hours; existing recording tools. | Check completion of the video and download intent; never shorten actual latency deceptively. |
| Guided install versus self-serve install | Ten consenting sessions; five hours. | Identify repeated failures and fix the largest obstruction before broader distribution. |
| Two search pages | Six to eight hours; existing hosting. | Review indexing/query relevance at 30 days and qualified traffic at 60; no immediate ranking promise. |
| Small meetup demonstration | One tailored application and preparation; no sponsorship purchase. | Useful conversations and subsequent activated testers; log effort even if no users result. |
| Curator pitch after proof exists | Up to five individualized future pitches; two hours. | Coverage and qualified visitors, with no follow-up pressure or mass list. |
| Optional paid message test after retention | Proposed maximum $150 for one audience/creative test, only if later authorized. | Stop at the cap or broken attribution; inspect activation/return before considering any increase. |

For a free product, a dollar cost per activation alone does not establish a sustainable business. Decide the intended funding or pricing model before scaling spend. Paid user-research compensation, if later approved, should pay for participants' time regardless of whether their feedback is positive.

## The next five actions

1. Attach the actual release's platform and feature evidence to the claims table; distinguish beta from verified support.
2. Observe ten installations and measure completion time before selecting the main speed claim.
3. Produce a continuous wording-preservation demo and a realistic coding-prompt demo.
4. Publish a clear download route plus two useful search pages, then share one relevant demonstration through an existing audience.
5. Review activation and first-week return; use the results to decide the next launch and feature priorities.

The practical success criterion is a growing group of people who choose Parla again because it handles their speech and their workflow well. A star increase is welcome evidence of attention; it does not substitute for that result.
